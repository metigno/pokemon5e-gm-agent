# ASTERIA FAUNA — RARITY SYSTEM

## Scope

Every canonical Pokémon record currently exposed by Pokémon 5e is represented in Asteria's fauna registry.

Unofficial/Fakémon records are excluded.

The registry is **not** a copy of Pokémon 5e stat blocks. It stores only Asteria-specific distribution metadata.

## Ordinary wild rarity

| Tier | Pool weight | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Common | 100 | routinely present in suitable habitat |
| Uncommon | 45 | present but not expected every outing |
| Rare | 15 | meaningful sighting |
| Very Rare | 5 | requires patience, conditions or luck |
| Exceptional | 1 | mature/scarce apex or extremely unusual wild sighting |
| Protected Rare | 2 | rare protected starter-family population |
| Protected Very Rare | 1 | evolved protected population |

Weights are relative inside a valid local pool. They are **not percentages**.

A GM still checks time, weather, habitat, current events and Pokémon behavior before selecting an encounter.

## How rarity was built

Rarity is derived from:
- Pokémon 5e encounter difficulty / minimum-level profile;
- evolution stage;
- local Asteria ecology;
- special protection for starter families;
- scarcity for pseudo-legendary families.

No level scaling changes a species' ecological rarity.

## Special classes

These never use ordinary random weights:

- **Paleo** — fossil recovery, revival or managed rewilding.
- **Legendary Unique** — story/interregional unique encounter only.
- **Mythical Unique** — story/interregional unique encounter only.
- **Ultra Rift** — Ultra Beast event only.
- **Anomaly** — Paradox event only.
- **Unique Event** — special non-standard case.

## Evolution stages in the wild

Evolution does not mean a species disappears from nature.

Mature stages can exist in the same ecological family, but their rarity rises naturally through higher encounter difficulty and evolution-stage logic.

Seeing a final evolution in the wild should therefore feel materially different from finding its first stage.

## Starter families

Starter species are not common merely because players often receive them.

Asteria maintains small protected or long-established populations in suitable habitats.

They can be encountered in the world, but normally at Rare+ tiers.

## Pseudo-legendary families

Breeding populations exist, but are deliberately sparse.

Early stages are rare; mature stages become extremely uncommon.

## Regional forms

Alolan, Galarian, Hisuian and Paldean forms that have a valid ecological niche use localized Asteria micro-populations.

They do not replace the base form across the entire territory.
