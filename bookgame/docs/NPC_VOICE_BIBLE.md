# P5E LIBROGAME — NPC VOICE BIBLE V1

**Status:** NARRATIVE PRODUCTION STANDARD  
**Scope:** recurring NPC voice, with M01 production profiles locked first  
**Companion document:** `NARRATIVE_BIBLE.md`

---

# 0. PRIMARY RULE

A character seed defines how a member of the Five behaves **only while that character is an NPC**.

If that character is selected as the player:

- do not author their dialogue;
- do not force their emotions;
- do not force their tactical personality;
- do not narrate their internal thoughts;
- present choices and let the player define the protagonist.

The Five remain recognizable as NPCs without turning player selection into a personality prison.

---

# 1. VOICE DIMENSIONS

Every persistent NPC should be defined across:

- sentence length;
- directness;
- emotional openness;
- humor;
- competitive intensity;
- preferred vocabulary;
- how they disagree;
- how they show respect;
- how they show concern;
- what they avoid saying;
- how their voice changes under pressure.

Do not reduce voice to catchphrases.

Repeated catchphrases make characters feel synthetic.

# 2. THE FIVE — ROOKIE VOICES

## 2.1 Luke — NPC only

**Core seed:** instinctive, competitive, energetic, adaptable, emotionally invested in his Pokémon.

### Voice
- short-to-medium sentences;
- action-first;
- concrete rather than theoretical;
- competitive without constant boasting;
- willing to admit a bad call after the fact more easily than before it.

### Typical rhythm
Luke often:
1. identifies the immediate thing to do;
2. proposes action;
3. adjusts quickly when reality changes.

### Respect
He shows respect by:
- asking for another match;
- trusting someone with a real task;
- acknowledging a clever adjustment;
- becoming less performative and more focused.

### Concern
He shows concern through action:
- checks a Pokémon;
- offers to help;
- stays nearby;
- suggests moving now rather than discussing feelings for ten minutes.

### Avoid
- making him reckless in every scene;
- making him stupid because he is instinctive;
- generic shōnen shouting;
- constant “I’ll beat everyone” lines.

### Example flavor
> «Se restiamo qui a parlarne, le tracce spariscono. Io vado a vedere dove portano.»

---

## 2.2 Mattew — NPC only

**Core seed:** methodical, disciplined, pattern-focused, calculated risk.

### Voice
- precise;
- measured;
- rarely wastes words;
- asks clarifying questions;
- notices sequence, timing and inconsistencies.

### Typical rhythm
Mattew often:
1. separates facts from assumptions;
2. identifies what can be tested;
3. commits once the choice is good enough.

### Respect
He shows respect by:
- sharing preparation;
- asking for the player's read;
- adjusting a plan based on their input;
- treating them as a serious benchmark.

### Concern
He becomes more specific, not more emotional:
> «Hai ancora due ore prima della chiusura. Se vuoi andare, fallo dopo aver curato il Pokémon.»

### Avoid
- robot voice;
- spreadsheet jokes every scene;
- omniscient prediction;
- analysis so perfect it eliminates uncertainty.

### Example flavor
> «Tre segnalazioni nello stesso tratto non sono una coincidenza. Non sappiamo ancora se hanno la stessa causa.»

---

## 2.3 Daniel — NPC only

**Core seed:** layered planner, reads intentions, sees branches, prone to over-analysis.

### Voice
- medium sentences;
- often frames alternatives;
- enjoys implication;
- dry humor is allowed;
- may test an idea aloud before committing.

### Typical rhythm
Daniel often:
1. offers an obvious explanation;
2. points out the second possibility;
3. occasionally invents a third possibility that is too clever.

### Respect
He shows respect by:
- revealing the line of reasoning he normally keeps back;
- asking what the other person thinks he missed;
- accepting a simple explanation when the evidence supports it.

### Concern
He tries to foresee what could go wrong and may sound more detached than he is.

### Avoid
- making him mysterious for no reason;
- villain-coded manipulation;
- always being correct;
- endless rhetorical questions.

### Example flavor
> «Potrebbe essere un animale spaventato. Oppure qualcuno vuole che sembri un animale spaventato. Prima controlliamo le impronte.»

---

## 2.4 Edward — NPC only

**Core seed:** direct, aggressive, resilient, learns by doing.

### Voice
- short;
- physical;
- candid;
- low patience for theory without a next action;
- humor can be blunt but not cruel.

### Typical rhythm
Edward often:
1. asks what needs doing;
2. tries it;
3. learns from the result.

### Respect
He shows respect openly:
- “bella mossa”;
- rematch offers;
- trusts someone to take the harder side;
- does not hide when he was beaten.

### Concern
He is straightforward:
> «Quel Pokémon zoppica. Prima quello, poi il resto.»

### Avoid
- dumb brute stereotype;
- aggression toward every NPC;
- treating all problems as battles;
- inability to learn strategy.

### Example flavor
> «Possiamo discutere il percorso per mezz'ora, oppure provarne uno e vedere dove ci porta.»

---

## 2.5 Fab — NPC only

**Core seed:** patient, tenacious, affectionate toward Pokémon, comfortable with gradual progress.

### Voice
- calm;
- slightly slower rhythm;
- practical;
- notices condition and endurance;
- rarely competes for conversational dominance.

### Typical rhythm
Fab often:
1. observes;
2. gives the situation time;
3. acts decisively once the window is clear.

### Respect
He shows respect by:
- entrusting care;
- sharing space quietly;
- acknowledging consistency rather than spectacle.

### Concern
He checks condition, fatigue, food, recovery and stress.

### Avoid
- passive personality;
- saintly animal-lover stereotype;
- making him afraid of conflict;
- turning patience into indecision in every scene.

### Example flavor
> «Aspetta un momento. Non sta venendo verso di noi. Sta cercando un'altra uscita.»

# 3. BLUE — M01 ANCHOR

**M01 identity:** young, proud, competitive, technically serious, still early in his career.

Blue is not yet the mature future trainer and must not speak as if he already owns that history.

## Voice
- concise;
- confident;
- lightly provocative;
- specific when discussing battle;
- rarely sentimental;
- does not need to insult people to be competitive.

## What he wants in M1
To assess:
- who is serious;
- who wastes time;
- who can separate intention from result;
- who adapts after being tested.

## Respect
Blue becomes:
- less dismissive;
- more technically specific;
- more willing to offer a real match or useful information.

He should almost never say:
> «Ti rispetto.»

Show it through behavior.

## After a Blue win
No gloating monologue.

Possible texture:
> «Non male. Ma hai lasciato il campo aperto due volte.»  
> Blue richiama Squirtle e controlla l'ora prima di aggiungere: «La prossima sarà diversa.»

## After a Blue loss
No humiliation and no instant friendship.

Possible texture:
> Blue resta zitto il tempo necessario a richiamare Squirtle. «Quella seconda scelta era giusta.» Fa una pausa. «Non contare sul fatto che funzioni uguale la prossima volta.»

## If ignored
Blue does not become offended merely because the protagonist had other priorities.

His career continues.

## Avoid
- cartoon arrogance;
- constant “smell ya later”-style imitation;
- villain energy;
- exposition delivery;
- acting as M1's final boss.

# 4. M01 LOCAL CAST

## 4.1 Sera Noll — Job Board coordinator

### Function
Makes work feel like part of a living local economy.

### Voice
- efficient;
- brisk;
- remembers reliability;
- does not oversell jobs;
- separates “available” from “good idea”.

### Respect
Offers clearer information and may trust the player with time-sensitive details.

### Friction
If the player wastes a slot or repeatedly abandons work, Sera becomes procedural rather than hostile.

### Avoid
- quest-giver clichés;
- “hero, save us” language;
- unexplained reward dumps.

### Example
> «Due incarichi sono ancora aperti. Quello ai magazzini scade prima. La fattoria paga meno, ma non ha una finestra così stretta.»

---

## 4.2 Toma Ferren — Trainer Shop

### Voice
- practical merchant;
- fast mental arithmetic;
- knows what rookie trainers usually forget;
- does not lecture unless asked.

### Respect
Stops giving beginner assumptions once the player proves competent.

### Avoid
- comedy salesman stereotype;
- tutorial dump;
- telling the player what to buy.

### Example
> «Posso vendertelo. Non significa che ti serva oggi. Cosa stai preparando?»

---

## 4.3 Dott.ssa Mira Venn — Pokémon Center

### Voice
- calm;
- clinical without being cold;
- concise;
- prioritizes condition over competition.

### Respect
Uses more precise medical language as the player becomes experienced.

### Avoid
- motherly infantilization;
- magical-healing prose that contradicts rules;
- moral judgment over legal battle outcomes.

### Example
> «Prima controllo il fianco. Poi parliamo di quando può tornare in campo.»

---

## 4.4 Bram Alder — warehouse logistics

### Voice
- worker-first;
- terse;
- dislikes wasted motion;
- knows routes, timings, deliveries and practical constraints.

### Respect
Shows it by giving responsibility, not compliments.

### Avoid
- gruff caricature;
- hidden-villain hints in M1;
- treating ecology as irrelevant stupidity.

### Example
> «Quel passaggio era libero la settimana scorsa. Ora ogni secondo carrello fa il giro lungo.»

---

## 4.5 Ranger Elio Mar — urban wildlife

### Voice
- evidence-first;
- calm;
- ecological vocabulary kept understandable;
- does not moralize a single encounter without context.

### Typical rhythm
1. asks what was actually observed;
2. separates behavior from interpretation;
3. connects patterns only with enough evidence.

### Respect
He asks the player for observations rather than simply instructing them.

### Avoid
- “Pokémon good / humans bad” simplification;
- omniscience;
- treating one encounter as proof of a regional theory.

### Example
> «Dimmi cosa ha fatto, non cosa pensi volesse fare. Distanza, postura, via di fuga. Partiamo da lì.»

---

## 4.6 Nara Voss — F→E examiner

### Voice
- neutral;
- economical;
- professional;
- not cruel;
- not encouraging for the sake of encouragement.

### Before Trial
Explains only what the candidate needs to know.

### After win
Acknowledges promotion as earned, not destiny.

### After loss
Treats failure as recorded experience, not humiliation.

### Avoid
- boss speech;
- taunting;
- secret scaling hints;
- emotional speeches after every attempt.

### Example win
> «Esito valido. Rank E.» Nara aggiorna il registro e ti restituisce il documento. «Da qui in poi cambia chi accetta di affrontarti. Preparati di conseguenza.»

### Example loss
> «Tentativo registrato.» Nara chiude il terminale. «Puoi presentarti di nuovo quando vuoi, se i requisiti restano validi.»

# 5. RELATIONSHIP EXPRESSION

Relationship numbers remain engine data.

Never render:
- +4 relationship;
- friendly state;
- rivalry score.

Render relationship through changes in behavior.

## Low / distant
- shorter answers;
- formal address;
- less volunteered information;
- fewer personal invitations.

## Developing respect
- more direct challenge;
- more specific information;
- willingness to share time;
- callbacks to prior actions.

## Strong relationship
- private context;
- greater trust;
- stronger emotional stakes;
- more willingness to disagree honestly.

Strong relationship does not mean universal agreement.

---

# 6. RESULT CALLBACKS

NPCs should react to real outcomes.

## Win
Do not automatically make the NPC impressed.

Reaction depends on:
- personality;
- stakes;
- relationship;
- quality of the situation they actually observed.

## Loss
Do not erase it.

A loss may produce:
- teasing;
- technical advice;
- concern;
- indifference;
- opportunity;
- rivalry.

## Declining a battle
Never frame this automatically as cowardice.

An NPC may interpret it based on their personality, but the narrator remains neutral.

---

# 7. SPEECH TAGS AND BODY LANGUAGE

Use speech tags lightly.

Prefer:
> Blue guarda il campo. «Uno contro uno?»

Over:
> «Uno contro uno?» dice Blue competitivamente.

Let action carry tone.

Body language should be:
- observable;
- specific;
- non-telepathic.

Avoid:
> Mira looks secretly worried.

Prefer:
> Mira reads the same line on the monitor twice before answering.

---

# 8. EVOLUTION OF VOICE ACROSS MODULES

Recurring NPCs may mature.

Changes must come from actual career events.

Examples:
- rookie confidence becomes professional precision;
- early over-analysis becomes better prioritization;
- direct aggression becomes controlled pressure;
- patience becomes strategic discipline.

Do not jump a character from rookie voice to champion voice merely because a later module begins.

# 10. VOICE QA

Before approving dialogue ask:

1. Could this line be spoken unchanged by three other characters?
   - If yes, sharpen voice or cut it.
2. Is the NPC explaining something only because the author needs exposition?
   - If yes, motivate or relocate it.
3. Does the line know more than the NPC can know?
   - If yes, rewrite.
4. Does the line expose relationship/state mechanics?
   - If yes, rewrite diegetically.
5. If the character is the selected protagonist, did authored personality leak into player speech?
   - If yes, remove it.
6. Does the reaction honor the actual prior result?
   - If no, fix continuity.
